Between 46% and 53% of the nodes the benchmark counts as
recovered do not carry the gold label: they match only above the
threshold. Node F1 therefore roughly halves under exact matching
(0.209-0.779 against 0.102-0.415).

This is a property of the instrument, not of any system: the ranking
of items is unchanged (Spearman 0.75-0.83, analysis B8). What it says
is what the reported score measures - that a reader of the figure can
find a node about the right thing, not that the label is the gold one.
Reported alongside the ceiling of 0.611 (N1) it closes the account of
why absolute scores on this benchmark are low and must stay low.

Two findings, both about the instrument and both usable by anyone who
runs this benchmark:

(a) The position bias is dimension-specific and its sign is not the
    same across dimensions of one judge: for InternVL3.5-14B it runs
    -12.4 points on faithfulness and +17.9 on readability.
    Averaging the two orders is therefore required per dimension; an
    overall average does not cancel it, because the dimensions do not
    move together.
(b) The dimension this literature would use as a legibility measure is
    the least reproducible one across judges: readability kappa = 0.151
    (47.4% agreement) against conciseness kappa = 0.294 (58.2%).
    Section 6 shows that dimension does not respond to the geometry
    a reader pays for; it also does not agree with a second judge.

== G3  The ceiling of any two-engine selector
==============================================================================
F3 gave 'dot' an oracle over its own four configurations. The next
question a caller asks is whether a *policy over both engines* beats
always using ours. The ceiling of every such policy is the figure
that is legible under either engine, so it is measurable here.

     corpus     model    n  ours  dot_oracle  both_oracle  headroom
    sciflow      Qwen 1416  93.1        80.7         96.3       3.1
    sciflow     Gemma 1416  91.4        71.5         93.7       2.3
    sciflow Ministral 1416  74.2        40.8         79.5       5.3
    flowvqa      Qwen  300  97.3        92.0         99.3       2.0
    flowvqa     Gemma  300  98.0        86.3         99.0       1.0
    flowvqa Ministral  300  87.7        67.7         89.0       1.3
paperbanana      Qwen  287  95.1        84.0         96.5       1.4
paperbanana     Gemma  292  94.5        64.0         96.2       1.7
paperbanana Ministral  285  57.5        21.1         61.4       3.9

Pooled over 6,012 figures: ours 86.9% strictly legible,
oracle 'dot' 65.9%, and the best of the two per figure
90.0%. Always using ours therefore reaches 96.6% of what
any engine-selection policy over these two engines could reach, while
always using 'dot' with an oracle over its configurations reaches 73.2%.
Discordant figures: ours only 1,451, oracle 'dot' only 185 (McNemar p = 1.29e-243).

The headroom of the perfect selector is 3.1 points pooled and at most
5.3 in any setting. The cheapest objection to a new layout engine
- "just pick the right existing engine per figure" - is worth less
than that, since no policy can exceed its oracle.

== G4  The engine-choice rule of F4, validated across corpora
==============================================================================
F4 fits box count, loop and container on all figures at once and
reports AUC in sample. A caller wants the rule on a corpus it was not
fitted on, so the fit is repeated holding one corpus out, with two
features added that cost nothing to compute (arrow count, density).

AUC of predicting that an engine will draw the figure illegibly,
from graph properties alone, with no layout run:
      held_out  n_test  F4_3feat  F4_5feat  oracle_5feat  ours_5feat
       sciflow    4248     0.762     0.789         0.823       0.816
       flowvqa     900     0.748     0.749         0.817       0.881
   paperbanana     864     0.793     0.805         0.849       0.879
in sample (F4)    6012     0.772     0.795         0.839       0.838

Held out, the rule keeps AUC 0.75-0.81 for 'dot' and 0.82-0.88 for ours, on
a corpus no figure of which was used to fit it. It is therefore a rule
and not a description of these runs: a caller holding an extracted
graph can decide, before drawing anything, whether the drawing needs
checking. Adding arrow count and density to F4's three features
raises the in-sample AUC from 0.772 to 0.795; F4 reports 0.772 for the three-feature fit on the same target, which this reproduces.
