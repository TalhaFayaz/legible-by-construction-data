== V5  arrow direction: an error class no graph score here separates
================================================================================
   a reversed edge is one the extractor found and pointed the wrong way.
   node-and-edge F1 scores it exactly like a missing edge, so the two errors --
   which call for different fixes -- are merged. Both are computable from any
   predicted graph, so any benchmark of this kind can separate them for free.

  corpus/source      model       rev/fig   %gold  figs>=1  %located  edgeF1  undir.   delta
  sciflow (papers)   Qwen          0.021   0.16%     1.8%     3.78%   0.028   0.029 +0.0011
  sciflow (papers)   Gemma         0.023   0.20%     2.1%     2.73%   0.040   0.041 +0.0011
  sciflow (papers)   Ministral     0.040   0.22%     2.4%     3.63%   0.038   0.040 +0.0014
  flowvqa code       Qwen          0.047   0.37%     4.7%     0.51%   0.726   0.729 +0.0037
  flowvqa code       Gemma         0.040   0.25%     3.0%     0.44%   0.727   0.730 +0.0032
  flowvqa code       Ministral     0.057   0.45%     4.3%     0.59%   0.736   0.741 +0.0044
  flowvqa instruct   Qwen          0.117   0.52%    10.0%     1.53%   0.413   0.420 +0.0064
  flowvqa instruct   Gemma         0.110   0.48%    10.0%     1.46%   0.389   0.395 +0.0058
  flowvqa instruct   Ministral     0.217   0.96%    16.7%     2.25%   0.391   0.400 +0.0090
  flowvqa wiki       Qwen          0.080   0.31%     7.7%     1.18%   0.358   0.362 +0.0043
  flowvqa wiki       Gemma         0.187   0.66%    16.7%     2.70%   0.340   0.350 +0.0094
  flowvqa wiki       Ministral     0.237   0.94%    20.0%     2.35%   0.401   0.411 +0.0097

  papers (sciflow)        2.1% of figures carry one [1.7,2.5], 3.36% of the relations it located, edge F1 0.036 -> 0.037 ignoring direction
  code                    4.0% of figures carry one [2.8,5.3], 0.51% of the relations it located, edge F1 0.730 -> 0.733 ignoring direction
  prose (wiki+instruct)  13.5% of figures carry one [11.9,15.1], 1.95% of the relations it located, edge F1 0.383 -> 0.391 ignoring direction
  prose / code ratio 1.8x per gold edge, Mann-Whitney p = 2.5e-14 (E4)
  on the 5247 figures whose graph is also in layout_items, a graph that loops back carries
  a reversed arrow on 5.0% of figures against 2.8% for one that does not (Mann-Whitney p = 3.1e-05),
  so the error concentrates where the extraction is hardest and the figure most needs
  to be read in order.

  the fix costs nothing: report a direction-insensitive edge F1 beside the directed
  one, or the reversal count itself. The gap between the two is the share of the
  benchmark's deficit that is a direction error and not a missing relation.

== V6  the corpus as a calibration set: the reader's transfer function, measured
================================================================================
   V1 shows the read-back reader does not return what was drawn. Because the drawn
   graph is known exactly, the map from what it returns to what is there can be
   fitted and tested out of sample: papers are split by position into two halves,
   the correction is fitted on one and scored on the other.

  model      target   slope     R2  MAE raw  MAE cal.  MAE const
  Qwen       boxes     0.45   0.40     6.08      3.97       5.58
  Qwen       arrows    0.28   0.26    11.83      4.60       5.50
  Gemma      boxes     0.43   0.62     5.19      3.48       5.70
  Gemma      arrows    0.35   0.47    12.72      4.08       5.82
  Ministral  boxes     0.33   0.32    11.98      6.10       7.99
  Ministral  arrows    0.26   0.28    18.39      6.25       7.35

  calibration roughly halves the error on arrows (11.8-18.4 arrows to 4.1-6.3),
  but the fitted slope is 0.26-0.35 rather than 1 and explains only 0.26-0.47 of the variance,
  so the corrected estimate is barely better than ignoring the reader and returning
  the mean of the corpus (last column).

  cross-model transfer of the arrow correction (MAE, all papers of the target model):
    fitted on Qwen       -> Qwen  4.47   Gemma  4.37   Ministral  8.60
    fitted on Gemma      -> Qwen  4.59   Gemma  4.11   Ministral  7.29
    fitted on Ministral  -> Qwen  8.34   Gemma  7.44   Ministral  6.55
  a correction fitted on one model's figures is worse on another's than that
  model's own, so the transfer function belongs to the figure set and not to the
  reader alone: it cannot be fitted once and carried to a new system's figures.

  what transfers is the procedure, not the coefficients: any corpus whose geometry
  is known lets a reader be audited and bounded before it is trusted to score a
  system, and this one shows the bound is not removable by calibration.

== V7  a judge verdict belongs to its dimensions, as a geometric verdict does
================================================================================
   PaperBananaBench combines four dimensions into one number: faithfulness and
   conciseness decide, readability and aesthetics break ties. Section 7 does not
   report a six-criterion verdict without attributing it to its criteria; this is
   the same operation on the judge, over the identical figures and both judges.

  InternVL3.5-14B  Gemma         -39.2    -17.4*        -34.8*         -6.2*        -15.8*    
  InternVL3.5-14B  Ministral     -22.6     -8.8*        -27.3*         -6.9*         -3.5

   in the CSV is a 95% bootstrap interval over the 292 items.)

  what share of the overall difference each loss's deciding dimension accounts for:
    InternVL3.5-14B  Gemma      conciseness  -34.8 of overall  -39.2  =    89%
    InternVL3.5-14B  Ministral  conciseness  -27.3 of overall  -22.6  =   121%

    overall        moves  15.1 to  22.0 points, sign kept for 3 of 3 models
  the dimension that carries the verdict, conciseness, is the one the change of
  judge leaves alone; the overall number moves further than any dimension in it.

  how often each dimension separates the two figures at all:
    faithfulness   ties on 62.8% [62.1,63.5] of judgements   model 13.9%   human 23.2%
    conciseness    ties on 23.4% [22.8,24.0] of judgements   model 20.5%   human 56.0%
    readability    ties on 65.1% [64.4,65.8] of judgements   model 10.1%   human 24.6%
    aesthetics     ties on 48.1% [47.3,48.8] of judgements   model 15.4%   human 36.4%
  the dimension that names legibility is silent on two judgements in three; the

  generated figure. These shares match the tie column of checks_judge.py --analysis

  0.294 against readability 0.151, the two ends of the same ranking.

  and what each dimension responds to, inside one system, per figure:
    InternVL3.5-14B  Qwen       n= 291  fait -0.23  conc -0.42  read -0.45  aest -0.22  over -0.35  
    InternVL3.5-14B  Gemma      n= 292  fait -0.22  conc -0.40  read -0.39  aest -0.22  over -0.38  
    InternVL3.5-14B  Ministral  n= 290  fait -0.23  conc -0.34  read -0.37  aest -0.33  over -0.30  
    Qwen2.5-VL-32B   Qwen       n= 291  fait -0.06  conc -0.38  read -0.26  aest -0.11  over -0.27  
    Qwen2.5-VL-32B   Gemma      n= 292  fait -0.00  conc -0.25  read -0.14  aest -0.19  over -0.18  
    Qwen2.5-VL-32B   Ministral  n= 290  fait -0.02  conc -0.37  read -0.29  aest -0.20  over -0.26  
  every dimension falls with the number of boxes the figure holds, the deciding one
  fastest (rho -0.25 to -0.42). Section 6 shows the same slope between twelve
  systems; here it is inside one system, figure by figure. With size held fixed the
  readability dimension tracks no geometry at all (C1), so it reads how much a
  figure holds and not whether it can be read.

  what transfers: report a judge per dimension and attribute the verdict before
  reporting it. Here that turns one unstable number into one stable finding (the
  generated figures are less concise) and three unstable ones.

== V8  the geometric signal is stable in aggregate and noisy per figure, which is what selection spends
================================================================================
   the layout is a deterministic function of the graph, so everything that moves
   between two seeds of one item is the extraction. V3 sells the counts as a
   best-of-n signal; this is the reliability of that signal and the ceiling of
   acting on it, measured over three seeds of the shipped pipeline.

  corpus       model          n   flips  seed spread    mean  best of 3  worst of 3   ceiling
  sciflow      Qwen         472   11.2%         0.7    93.2       97.8        86.8     +4.6 [+3.4,+5.9]
  sciflow      Gemma        472    9.3%         1.7    91.4       95.4        86.2     +4.1 [+2.9,+5.3]
  sciflow      Ministral    472   34.1%         3.0    74.3       89.0        55.4    +14.7 [+12.6,+16.7]
  flowvqa      Qwen         300    4.3%         1.0    97.7       99.7        95.3     +2.0 [+1.0,+3.2]
  flowvqa      Gemma        300    2.7%         0.7    98.0       99.0        96.3     +1.0 [+0.3,+1.8]
  flowvqa      Ministral    300   18.0%         4.7    90.8       98.0        80.0     +7.2 [+5.4,+9.2]
  paperbanana  Qwen         287    9.4%         1.7    95.2       99.3        89.9     +4.1 [+2.6,+5.7]
  paperbanana  Gemma        291   12.0%         1.7    93.5       98.3        86.3     +4.8 [+3.3,+6.4]
  paperbanana  Ministral    281   53.7%         0.7    58.0       84.7        31.0    +26.7 [+23.5,+29.9]

  the seed moves the pass rate of a setting by 0.7 to 4.7 points and the verdict of a figure on
  2.7 to 53.7% of them: the aggregate is reproducible and a single figure's verdict is not,
  so one run under-reports a figure's legibility by up to 27.0 points of its own corpus.

  and the share of figures whose verdict is seed-dependent is what one extra
  sample buys, so the headroom can be read off a few resampled items in advance:
    sciflow  Qwen       verdict seed-dependent on  11.2% of figures   measured best-of-2  +5.9 pts   best-of-3 ceiling  +4.6 pts
    sciflow  Gemma      verdict seed-dependent on   9.3% of figures   measured best-of-2  +6.5 pts   best-of-3 ceiling  +4.1 pts
    sciflow  Ministral  verdict seed-dependent on  34.1% of figures   measured best-of-2 +17.3 pts   best-of-3 ceiling +14.7 pts
    flowvqa  Qwen       verdict seed-dependent on   4.3% of figures   measured best-of-2  +3.3 pts   best-of-3 ceiling  +2.0 pts
    flowvqa  Gemma      verdict seed-dependent on   2.7% of figures   measured best-of-2  +4.3 pts   best-of-3 ceiling  +1.0 pts
    flowvqa  Ministral  verdict seed-dependent on  18.0% of figures   measured best-of-2 +17.4 pts   best-of-3 ceiling  +7.2 pts
    rank correlation over the 6 settings: rho = +0.83 (p = 0.042)

  the same instability is there on content and cannot be spent:
    sciflow  Qwen       score 0.104, seed spread 0.000, best of three 0.117 (+0.014)
    sciflow  Gemma      score 0.124, seed spread 0.002, best of three 0.131 (+0.008)
    sciflow  Ministral  score 0.124, seed spread 0.002, best of three 0.139 (+0.015)
    flowvqa  Qwen       score 0.600, seed spread 0.006, best of three 0.646 (+0.046)
    flowvqa  Gemma      score 0.587, seed spread 0.002, best of three 0.614 (+0.027)
    flowvqa  Ministral  score 0.639, seed spread 0.007, best of three 0.682 (+0.042)
  picking the best of three samples would add 0.008 to 0.046 of score as well, and
  nothing available at generation time says which sample that is: a defect is
  countable per figure and a missing relation is not. That asymmetry, and not the
  size of either headroom, is why selection by geometry is free and selection on
  content needs a gold graph or a judge.
